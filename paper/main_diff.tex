\subsection*{Channel, feasible decisions and opportunity loss}
\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{A receiver }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{We consider a base station }\DIFaddend with \(N\) jointly processed antenna streams \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{serves }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{serving }\DIFaddend \(K=2S\) users on \(S\) labelled, orthogonal tones of equal bandwidth. For the two users \(i,j\) on tone \(s\), the whitened observation is \(y_s=\sqrt{p_i}h_{is}x_i+\sqrt{p_j}h_{js}x_j+n_s\), where the complex noise has covariance \(I_N\). In simulation, powers satisfy \(0.05\leq p_i\leq0.20\) W and \(\sum_i p_i\leq P=0.10K\) W. The Rice \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{replay instead uses }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{recordings use }\DIFaddend digital receiver units. The oracle serves every user once, optimizes within these power limits and uses a separate linear combiner for each user, treating its partner as interference.

For nonzero \(h_{is}\), Cauchy--Schwarz in the interference-plus-noise inner product gives the optimal combiner direction \((I_N+p_j h_{js}h_{js}^{H})^{-1}h_{is}\). Let \(x=p_i\|h_{is}\|^2\) and \(y=p_j\|h_{js}\|^2\). The squared normalized overlap \(r=|h_{is}^{H}h_{js}|^2/(\|h_{is}\|^2\|h_{js}\|^2)\) is set to zero if either channel vanishes. \begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_i &= x\left(1-\frac{r y}{1+y}\right), &
\gamma_j &= y\left(1-\frac{r x}{1+x}\right),\\
q_{e,s} &= \ln(1+\gamma_i)+\ln(1+\gamma_j), & e&=\{i,j\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sinr}
\end{equation}
The oracle rate \(V(h)\) maximizes the sum of pair rates over a finite set of schedules and a compact set of powers. Evaluating the stored filters at the implemented powers gives the Gaussian-input mutual information \({R_{\mathrm{fix}}}(h)\), assuming that the decoder knows the effective scalar complex gain \(v_i^Hh_{is}\) after combining, even when those filters are suboptimal. \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{On any channel for which both endpoints hold, \(U-L=(U-V)+(V-{R_{\mathrm{fix}}})+({R_{\mathrm{fix}}}-L)\). \subsection*{Building and checking the interval}
The input is a complex \(K\times N\times S\) array with uncertainties in whitened units. We round channel centres to a common dyadic grid with integer components bounded by \(2^{22}\). For \(N\leq256\), each real or imaginary Gram sum fits the exact integer range through \(2^{53}\). We enlarge the supplied vector radius by an outward bound \(\sqrt{2N}\,\delta/2\) on the Euclidean rounding displacement, where \(\delta\) is the grid spacing. For centres \(a,b\) and radii \(u,v\), the magnitude of their inner product is at least \(\left[|a^Hb|-\|a\|v-\|b\|u-uv\right]_{+}\). Dividing its square by the product of upper squared norms gives a lower bound on the squared normalized overlap when that product is positive. A zero product gives overlap zero. Bounding (\ref{eq:sinr}) with upper desired power and gain, lower interfering power and gain, and lower correlation encloses every realization in a power rectangle. We subtract the minimum power charge, and additionally take the better of that enclosure and a verified bound using tangents to the two individual concave log terms with a separately bounded correlation penalty.

\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{Every pair and tone receives an upper }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{Before refining any entry, we }\DIFaddend bound \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{on }\DIFdelend the \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{entire }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{full }\DIFaddend two-user power rectangle \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{before refinement begins}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{for every pair and tone}\DIFaddend . A change in price can reuse an unpriced enclosure. For each edge the two highest candidate tones enter a refinement shortlist. No more than \(\max(2,\lfloor K^2/32\rfloor)\) entries are fully divided into 16 rectangles. After all children cover a complete partition, the maximum child upper bound may tighten the parent.
